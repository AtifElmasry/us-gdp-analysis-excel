# Findings and interpretation

## 1. Largest and smallest sectors

With the original GDP grouping, Manufacturing ranked first in 1987. Finance ranked first in 1992 and 1997. Education ranked last in those three comparison years.

## 2. GDP shares vs nominal dollars

Finance increased from about 18% to 20% of GDP between 1987 and 1997, while Manufacturing decreased from about 19% to 17%. These shifts are **percentage-point changes** in share. Manufacturing's falling *share* does not mean that the dollar value of manufacturing GDP decreased.

## 3. Computer-related industries

The exercise reclassified `Computer and electronic products` and `Computer systems design and related services` into a new category named `Computers`. This category ranked 12th in 1987 and 10th in 1997. Reclassification changed the 1987 leadership order: Finance overtook Manufacturing.

## 4. Limitations

The analysis uses nominal dollars and a limited 1987–1997 window. Only industries explicitly mentioning computers were recoded, leaving much of IT's broader impact unmeasured. Classifications affect rankings, and descriptive analysis cannot prove causality.
